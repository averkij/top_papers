Abstract R²Orch uses recursive self-play with learnable task selection and skill withdrawal to train orchestrators without hand-designed curricula, improving performance across reasoning and interactive tasks.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small In recent years, agentic orchestration has been widely applied to solve complex human tasks. However, existing orchestration learning still faces key challenges, including hand-designed curricula, advantage collapse when a task's rollouts all reach the same outcome, and unreliable evaluation signals that judge each skill by one noisy score. To address these challenges, we propose R^2Orch, a new paradigm of Recursive Self-Play Orchestration: an inner loop of self-play that improves the orchestrator on self-chosen tasks, and an outer loop that improves how the inner loop improves. To support this paradigm, we introduce Recursive Learnability, a training objective that rewards a Proposer for choosing tasks whose rollouts keep reaching different outcomes as skills are withdrawn. Built on this objective, we further propose Skill Withdrawal, in which the outer loop withdraws each skill once a controlled evaluation within self-play shows that the orchestrator performs as well without it. Moreover, the two loops form one cycle: failures are distilled into skills, skills into parameters, and withdrawals lead the Proposer to new failures, so the orchestrator moves toward zero-skill deployment. Experimental results on twelve datasets show that R^2Orch consistently outperforms baselines across question answering, math, code, and interactive tasks. Our code is available at https://github.com/beita6969/R2Orch.

Abstract HiFOR improves robotic manipulation by hierarchically predicting future states and retrieving relevant future information to guide action generation.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated promising capabilities in robotic manipulation by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action generation. Recent advances have further incorporated future prediction to enhance the understanding of environmental dynamics and task progression. However, existing methods often model short-term dynamics and long-term task objectives separately, while directly incorporating predicted futures into action policies without sufficiently considering their relevance to current action requirements. To address these limitations, we propose HiFOR, a hierarchical foresight framework with action-guided world retrieval. Specifically, Hierarchical Foresight Prediction jointly predicts short-term state evolution, semantic milestones, and final goal states through a Hierarchical Dependency Mask (HDM), establishing dependencies across temporal horizons. Building upon these predictions, Structure-Aware Latent World Modeling organizes future latent representations along the horizon, view, and spatial dimensions to facilitate fine-grained retrieval. Furthermore, Action-Guided Factorized World Retrieval adaptively retrieves relevant future information from the structured latent world based on evolving action representations during denoising, providing targeted guidance for action generation. Extensive experiments on two simulation benchmarks and six real-world robotic manipulation tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach.

Our study highlights the complementary roles of video prediction and action grounding in developing robots that generalize across tasks.

Abstract CEPO improves multi-agent prompt optimization by separating shared coordination from agent-specific execution, boosting performance across benchmarks and scaling efficiently with more agents.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Multi-agent systems (MAS) require both effective coordination and specialized task execution. Existing prompt optimization approaches typically couple these functions within agent-specific prompts. We introduce Coordinator-Executor Prompt Optimization (CEPO), a general framework for improving prompt optimization in MAS. CEPO separates system-level coordination from agent-level execution through a shared coordinator prompt and agent-specific executor prompts, and guides their updates according to their respective roles. This structure allows shared coordination rules to be refined consistently across agents while keeping execution instructions separately adjustable. CEPO provides the prompt structure and optimization guidance, while existing prompt optimizers perform the underlying search. Extensive experiments spanning 11 benchmarks across four domains demonstrate that applying CEPO to four existing prompt optimizers consistently improves their performance. Ablations and qualitative analyses support the proposed design, while controlled studies on a document-understanding MAS further demonstrate improved optimization budget efficiency and performance scaling with the number of agents.
