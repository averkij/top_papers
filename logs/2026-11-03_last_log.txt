Introduction Softmax attention [1] remains substantial cost in Transformer training and inference, particularly at long context lengths. FlashAttention [2] reduces memory traffic through tiled computation and online softmax. FlashAttention-4 [3] further optimizes attention for NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs, but its BF16 implementation leaves higher low-precision throughput unexploited: Blackwell tensor cores support FP8 and FP4 matrix multiplication at twice and four times the BF16 rate, respectively [4]. Realizing these gains requires addressing quantization overhead and training numerics, while supporting the variable-length documents packed together during pretraining. Recent 4-bit extensions of FlashAttention-4 [5, 6] motivate examining these requirements beyond single-step accuracy. Three constraints are central. First, hardware block scaling follows the contraction axis, whereas attention training uses operands along different contraction axes, potentially requiring separately quantized representations. Second, scales shared across positions can let future tokens affect earlier outputs despite causal mask. Third, the backward pass must consistently reconstruct the forwards quantized intermediates: mismatches in quantization, tiling, or exponential evaluation can destabilize training despite high single-step cosine similarity. Packed inputs additionally require document-local scaling and load balancing. Among the evaluated implementations, some provide only 1 forward path for packed inputs [7], use tensor-wide FP8 scaling [8], or lack causal-mask support [9]. The mixed path uses NVFP4 for QK and its recomputation, and 8-bit operands for the remaining matrix multiplications. Token-block scaling enables reuse of 8-bit representations across orientations. FLAMIA combines targeted leakage mitigations with consistent forward and backward computations and processes packed documents without repacking. Default scaling retains within-block dependencies and sequence-wide backward accumulator dependency. Exact options remove them in supported batched causal calls.
